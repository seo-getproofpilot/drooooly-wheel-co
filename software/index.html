The shopper sees how far the wheel sits past the fender

Poke is the question every lifted-truck buyer asks, and a photo cannot answer it. A pin on the front wheel names the size, offset and finish, then says in one sentence how far the wheel sits past the fender at the lift they picked.

The stance meter in the panel runs from flush to full poke, so a 26x14 on a 4 inch lift and a 22x12 at stock height look different and read different.

Built for the phone your buyer has in their hand on the job site

What the configurator does not do

  1. It does not promise fitment. It shows the shopper a close picture of the build. Your team verifies the specs before anything is built or shipped, the same as today.
  2. It does not measure the shopper's truck. Stance is a preview stated in words, such as "sits about 6 inches past the fender", worked out from published sizes and offsets.
  3. It does not show the exact trim. The bodies are de-badged preview models. Wheelbase, track and tire size are real, and badges and trim details are left off.
  4. It does not cover every truck yet. Six crew-cab bodies are drawn today. Other trucks still go through your normal quote, and lifts taller than 6 inches are quoted by phone.
  5. It does not take payment. The build goes to your quote form, and your team closes the sale the way it already does.

# Selling tires without stocking them — which brands, how, and what each one makes

Chris: *"for you to be a dealer for Nitto Tires, they want you to order 24 sets at
a time … we are going to be on the e-commerce side first and not have a shop … go
through this in a deep dive and find exactly what we need for each tire dealer."*

Short version, before the detail:

- **The Nitto "24 sets" number is wrong, and it isn't the gate anyway.** It's
  **24 tires** (six sets), and it's the opening order for Nitto's *rebate* program,
  the Enthusiast Circuit. Anyone who buys Nitto from an authorized distributor is
  already an "Associate Dealer." You can sell Nitto with zero stock. What you can't
  get is the rebate: the program **excludes dealers that "primarily sell online."**
- **Every brand on our list can be bought one set at a time through a
  distributor.** No stock, no warehouse, no opening order of 24 of anything. The
  minimums people quote are for *rebate programs*, and online-only sellers mostly
  don't qualify for those anyway.
- **The real gate is online-selling rules, not volume.** **Toyo forbids selling on
  any website without written approval for that site.** Nitto and Mickey Thompson
  enforce strict MAP. Those are what can get an account cut off.
- **Venom Power and Fury are the right first two, and you called it.** They own the
  look this audience wants. They're the only brands that properly cover 24", 26",
  28" and 30" wheels. They publish no MAP. And both are close: **Venom's US owner is
  in Memphis**, and **Fury ships from one warehouse in Irving, TX.**
- **Neither publishes dealer terms.** Both run a "become a dealer" form with a rep
  call-back. Venom's form has an "under 1,000 tires a year" bracket, so small dealers
  are clearly expected. The numbers have to come from those two phone calls; §7
  has the script.
- **Tires are the traffic, wheels are the margin.** That already holds in
  `MARGINS.md`, and drop-ship makes it sharper. Expect **about 15–25% gross on the
  tire** before freight. A four-tire set nets roughly **$200–$550**, and a mounted
  package is where the money is.
- **The fastest no-inventory path is a distributor, not a brand.** Apply to
  **Turn 14** (accepts web-only dealers, drop-ships *mounted and balanced* packages)
  and **Keystone** (carries Fury and Falken, has a separate drop-ship account) while
  making the Venom and Fury calls.

Labels used throughout: **[O]** official (the brand's or distributor's own
document), **[R]** reported (trade press, retailer listing, forum), **[I]** inferred.
Search volumes are Google US monthly averages from DataForSEO, pulled 2026-10-11.

## 1. Why the "24 sets" fear doesn't apply to us

Three different things get called "becoming a dealer," and only one is required:

| | What it is | What it takes | Do we need it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Associate / authorized dealer** | You buy the brand from an authorized distributor and resell it | A distributor account, resale certificate, sales-tax permit | **Yes, and this is all we need to sell** |
| **Rebate program** (Nitto Enthusiast Circuit, Toyo Driven, Falken Fanatic, MT MTMA, Michelin AAD) | Per-tire cash back, co-op money, a dealer-locator listing | Opening orders and quarterly volume | **No.** Nice later. Online-only sellers mostly can't get in anyway |
| **Direct account** with the manufacturer | You buy from the brand itself by the truckload | Real volume, often a storefront | **No.** That's the stocking model we're avoiding |

So all three of your concerns hold, and none of them block us:
1. Tying up money in 24 sets makes no sense, and we don't have to.
2. Storing tires makes no sense, and the distributor or the brand holds them.
3. We won't have the volume yet, and drop-ship doesn't need any.

**What it costs us to skip the rebate programs:** $1.50–$10 a tire, so $6–$40 a
set [O]. That's real, but small against the $250–$550 gross on the set itself (§5).

### Nitto — sellable now, profitable later

- **Dealer:** buy from a direct Nitto distributor and you're an Associate Dealer [O].
  US AutoForce runs the program.
- **Rebates (Enthusiast Circuit):** a 24-tire opening order and 50+ a quarter, $6–$10
  a light-truck tire. **Dealers that "primarily sell online" are excluded, and
  installation service is required** [O].
- **MAP is strict (July 2024 policy) [O]:**
  - It applies to every online price shown before add-to-cart.
  - **"See price in cart," strike-through prices, and bundles that bring the
    effective price under MAP all count as violations.** That catches a
    "package discount" on a wheel-and-tire set.
  - "Call for price" is allowed.
  - Penalties run warning → violators list → permanent ban, and **distributors are
    barred from selling to anyone on the list.**
- **Demand is enormous:** Ridge Grappler **60,500/mo**, Mud Grappler 18,100, Trail
  Grappler 14,800, Recon Grappler 14,800. The Ridge Grappler is #1 on Custom Offsets'
  list and the default tire on 20x10 and 22x12 builds [O].
- **Verdict:** list it at MAP, through a distributor, and never discount it inside a
  package. It's the tire people search for by name. Expect thin margin (§5).

### Toyo — the one that needs permission first

- **Dealer:** buy from an authorized distributor and accept Toyo's dealer policy [O].
- **The gate: no selling on any website or marketplace without Toyo's written
  consent [O].** You apply through the "Authorized Online Seller Application"
  (ToyoDealerProgram@toyotires.com), and approval is **per website** [O].
- The application asks about drop-ship arrangements, warranty handling, the 45-day /
  500-mile trial and an installer network [O]. So drop-ship isn't automatically
  disqualifying, but it also asks for "warehouse facilities reasonably satisfactory
  to Toyo." **Expect pushback.**
- **Rebates (Driven):** 50-unit opening order, and **nothing on product "sold through
  the Internet"** [O].
- **Distributors:** Turn 14, TireHub, US AutoForce [R].
- **Demand:** Open Country R/T 9,900/mo, M/T 8,100. Open Country M/T in 37x13.50R22
  F (3,968 lb) is the premium pick on 22x14 [O].
- ⚠ **The site lists Toyo today.** See `LAUNCH-CHECKLIST.md` 3.6.

### Mickey Thompson — premium, MAP, storefront-minded

### BFGoodrich — big name, wrong channel for us

### Falken — the friendliest program to online sellers

- **Distributors:** Keystone, Meyer, US AutoForce.
- **Demand:** Wildpeak M/T 1,600/mo.

**Freight, which is the line that eats the margin:**
- Parcel shipping of a 35–37" tire takes **about $29–$33 a tire** in oversize
  surcharges alone [R].
- Freight carriers charge **up to $250 a set** for residential delivery, and some
  require a commercial address for anything over 35" [R].
- Plan **$150–$300 a set to a home**, less to a shop.

This is CLAUDE.md rule 6 in practice. **Freight is stated separately and is never
"free shipping."** The cheapest version is shipping to a partner installer near
the customer.

## 4. What's most popular, and why

### Overall

**The Nitto Ridge Grappler.** It has 60,500 searches a month, which is **more than
Venom Power, Fury, Toyo M/T, Toyo R/T, the Baja Boss and the KO3 put together.** It's
#1 on Custom Offsets' tire list [O], and the default tire on most 20" and 22" builds.

Why it wins:
- **The look:** a hybrid tread with aggressive shoulders and two different sidewall
  designs, so the buyer gets a choice.
- **It comes in load range F** in the big sizes.
- **It's quieter on the highway than a true mud tire**, which matters on a
  daily-driven truck.

### By category

| Category | Premium leader | Budget leader | Why the budget one sells |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hybrid / R/T** (what this audience mostly buys) | Nitto Ridge Grappler | **Venom Power Terra Hunter X/T** | Same look for roughly **$600 less a set**. Buyers put the money into the wheels |
| **Mud-terrain** | Toyo Open Country M/T, Mickey Thompson Baja Boss | **Gladiator X Comp M/T**, Fury Country Hunter MT2 | Big sidewall lugs, available in sizes premium brands don't make |
| **All-terrain** | BFGoodrich KO3, Toyo Open Country A/T III | Falken Wildpeak AT4W (mid-priced) | Less relevant here. A/T tires mostly stop at 20" |

**The pattern that matters for us:** above 24", the premium brands mostly stop
making tires. On 24", 26", 28" and 30" wheels, Fury, Venom, Atturo and Gladiator
aren't the "cheap option," **they're the only option.** That's why Venom and Fury
come first: they cover the wheels DROOOLY is built around.

### The towing problem, which is our hard stop

Bigger wheels mean less tire load capacity:
- A **37x13.50R26 F carries 2,835 lb**, less than a **35x12.50R20 E at 3,195 lb** [O].
- A stock HD tire (LT275/65R20 E) carries **about 3,640 lb**.
- **Most big-wheel lifted setups carry less per tire than the truck came with** [I].

That's exactly what CLAUDE.md rule 5's hard stop is for:
- The assembly rating is the **lower of the wheel and the tire**. Some 26x14s are
  rated only 2,500 lb [O].
- Load ratings must be stored **per part number**, not per tire size. Venom's
  35x12.50R22 E and F differ by 360 lb.
- **Never use a P-metric tire on an HD dually.** 305/35R24 carries about 2,470 lb [R].

## 5. What each tire makes us

**Planning numbers, not quotes.** No brand or distributor publishes dealer cost.
The margin bands come from industry figures and our position, not from a price
sheet:
- Installers report **25–40%** tire gross, but that includes service revenue [R].
- **Budget brands (Venom, Fury): 20–25% planned.** No published MAP, and these
  brands sell into price-led buyers.
- **Premium brands (Nitto, Toyo, Mickey Thompson): 15–20% planned.** MAP-capped,
  and no rebates for online sellers.

Every figure here is before freight and card fees.

selling tires as part of the package and not as a standalone tire store.

## 6. The order to do this in

1. **Call Transamerica (Venom Power), Memphis.** It's 3 hours away, small dealers
   are expected, and it's the brand most of our big-wheel builds will run.
2. **Submit Fury's distributor inquiry** *and* **open a Keystone drop-ship
   account.** If Fury's direct terms want volume, Keystone is the no-minimum way in.
3. **Apply to Turn 14.** One account covers Toyo, Mickey Thompson and BFG, and it
   ships **mounted and balanced** packages to the customer. That's our model. Ask
   about the buy-in first.
4. **List Nitto through a distributor at MAP, with no package discounting.**
   Revisit the Enthusiast Circuit once there's an installer partner.
5. **Send Toyo the Authorized Online Seller Application.** Keep Toyo off the site
   until it's approved.
6. **Skip BFGoodrich for now.** Michelin's program is built for stores, and the KO3
   isn't really a big-wheel tire.
7. **Hold AMP** until the A/T and R/T status is confirmed.
8. **Later: a connector** (Tireweb or Flxpoint) to reach Atturo and the long tail
   without opening a dozen accounts by hand.

## 7. Ask every rep the same questions

Written down because none of these answers are published.

1. **Can we buy with no opening order?** If not, what's the minimum, and is it
   waived for drop-ship?
2. **Do you need a storefront, or just a resale certificate and business license?**
3. **Do you drop-ship single sets to a consumer?** What's the fee per order and the
   freight per set, residential vs commercial? **Sets of six for duallys?**
4. **Do you mount and balance** onto wheels we send or source?
5. **What's dealer cost against your suggested retail** on our top four sizes? Are
   there volume tiers?
6. **Is there a written MAP or pricing policy?** Can we discount inside a
   wheel-and-tire package? Can we sell on Amazon or Walmart?
7. **How do warranty claims work for an online dealer with no shop?** Do you accept
   photos? (Venom and Fury both route claims back to the seller.)
8. **Does a fit inside your published rim-width range keep the warranty?**
9. **Lead time and stock reliability on 24", 26" and 28" sizes.**
10. **Load data per part number** (load index, max psi, dual rating, rim range) as a
    feed or spec sheet we can load into `data/tires/`.
11. **Can we use your product photos and logo?** We need that in writing for the
    brand wall (CLAUDE.md rule 3).
12. **Which distributors carry your full big-wheel range?**
